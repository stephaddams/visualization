#### Good Visualization  
[UNICEF's State of the World's Children Report 2016. By Yvan Fornes](https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/yvan.fornes/viz/UNICEFSOWC2016/StateoftheWorldsChildren)
       
```
Personally, I categorized this visualization as a good example due to its effectiveness in delivering the information to the user. It is functional, with a clean aesthetic, detailed, and interactive exploration, making it a strong example of a good and responsible data visualization.

First of all, I consider that the selection of a Geographical Heatmap was the most appropriate to represent the multiple samples of data on a global scale. The colors selected for the Heatmap are distinct enough to have a clear understanding of the values that they represent. 

In terms of design, it is clean and uncluttered. We have a header with a full description of the data source, as well as the period in use. The layout guides the user logically, reducing overload despite the complexity of the dataset. The consistent color palette and labeling maintain coherence throughout the visualization. 

As for readability, the visualization also includes a "how to read the graph" section, which is helpful to deeply understand the various measures shown. This makes the information accessible to a variety of types of final users.

Lastly, this visualization provides us with credibility in the information, as the sources are specified and linked for download, and the contact information of the responsible developer of the visualization is attached. We can see that the visualization represents the data honestly and transparently. 
        
Even I consider this visual already strong enough, some users may benefit from highlighted key findings or short contextual explanations that interpret major patterns, but as an overall view of the data, I consider that it does a great job. 
```

      
#### Bad Visualization     
[Carbon Emissions per capita by country. By David O Cinneide](https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/docinsight/viz/RadialMarimekko/RM)
        
```
This visualization is a poor example of data visualization because it fails in clarity, aesthetics, readability, and credibility, which ultimately limits its ability to communicate meaningful information. While the topic—carbon emissions per capita by country—is highly relevant, the execution weakens the message and makes interpretation unnecessarily difficult.        
        
First, the visualization lacks essential contextual information. It does not clearly state when the data was collected, what specific time period is being represented, what sources were used, or how the metric “carbon emissions per capita” is calculated. Without this information, users cannot assess the reliability or relevance of the data. Transparency is fundamental to credibility in data visualization. When users cannot verify sources or understand the methodology, trust in the results decreases. Additionally, at first glance, it is difficult to grasp what the chart is trying to communicate, which creates confusion rather than insight.
            
In terms of readability, the Radial Marimekko doesnt seems like a good option to represent data to compare multiple countries. It overlaps, becomes clustered, and is difficult to read, increasing the risk of misunderstanding. In a circular layout, there is no common baseline for comparing values. Also, the categories in which the countries are divided are not standard, as it's a categorization that tends to vary widely across different nations. (example, Oceania is not a part of Asia, but in this visualization is being cataloged as part of it). 
        
Aesthetically, the color palette does not effectively differentiate between categories such as “Middle East & Africa” and “Americas.” Similar tones reduce contrast and make distinctions harder to perceive, especially for users with color vision deficiencies.
        
Lastly, the main message of the visualization is unclear. Although the title establishes the “Carbon Emissions per capita by country” the chart does not clearly guide the viewer toward a clear takeaway. 

I consider that a way to improve the visualization is to implement the following:
    * Change the chart type to a Geographical Heatmap, and select a better color palette.
    * Include a note to specify how the total emissions per capita by country are being calculated.
    * Specify the date frame analyzed.
    * Link to the data sources utilized.
    * Enhance interactions (if digital). Adding tooltips with exact values.
    * Annotations pointing to notable outliers.
        
Overall, the current design fails to deliver a clear, trustworthy narrative, leaving the user with limited understanding.
```
